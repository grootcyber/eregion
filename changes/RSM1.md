### Added
- **Design: the reassertion's semantics written once** (RSM1, #1018; `docs/design/reaffirmation.md`). For each object Glorfindel places (an isolation by rules, the quarantine, a block of an IP on a VM, a perimeter block) and each situation (intact, gone, bypassed by a customer's rule, a lift under way, a quarantine that cannot be judged, an unreadable read, a shared or detached NSG, a deleted VM, a reassigned IP), a table says what the reassertion does: it puts back, it says (which card, which words), or it leaves, with its reason and its line of code.
  - The counterexamples of the nights of 10 to 20/11 are sorted: of the six causes that touched the reassertion, four were in the oracle (I9A, I3X, L1V, L1Nc) and two in the product (L1N, L1Nd). Each oracle defect redefined a word the code already decided otherwise: a lift that is over, a perimeter that cuts, a hold that is bypassed, a rule gone since an acknowledgement.
  - Five behaviours were measured on the in-memory Azure by a probe left out of the repository: a deleted VM's isolation or block (a card « non vérifiée », or « le reposer a échoué », nothing written), a block whose own deny is turned into an allow (said gone and rewritten, not bypassed), and a perimeter block whose subnet lost its NSG (said « autre subnet », a wrong cause).
  - For each situation, the invariants that hold it, and the seven that none holds. The gaps between the table, the code and the oracles, each with a proposal: correct the oracle (one reading of the hold in the test's world; the disappearance of an isolation judged after every pass; the exemptions read from the test's journal; the attacker's ways in `_attacker_flows`), correct the code (what a pass says of a VM that no longer exists, and of a subnet that lost its NSG), or write the rule. The lots that would follow, and seven questions for the maintainer. Linked from `docs/glorfindel.md` and `docs/design/preuves.md`. No code, no mutant.
