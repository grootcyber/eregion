### Added
- **The watch reads on Azure the recovery of a VM it isolated, and lifts the isolation itself** (REC1, #668; `docs/glorfindel.md`, « La reprise lue sur Azure »). H0 never restores (SEC17): a restore runs from the workstation, whose `recovery_complete` stays on that host, so H0's watch never released on its own, and its reassertion put back a release made elsewhere — two human gestures on the bench, restore then release. Every `GLORFINDEL_RECOVERY_INTERVAL_S` (60 s), for each isolation of its state, the watch reads through the adapter (`observed_restore`, `glorfindel/providers/azure/recovery.py`), never from another host's file nor from the model, and holds itself at least as strict as the workstation, which proves the neutralization by its own gesture and restores in place:
  - the VM's last restore job since its isolation, finished and `Completed`, in its container. A job naming no container counts only when it names its point by its id.
  - **A restore in place, by the control plane**: the job's type « Replace disks », and the VM's OS disk created during the job. A restore elsewhere (« Restore disks », a new VM) carries the same operation, name and container, yet leaves the isolated VM's disk: its card says not to release.
  - The point it restored, matched to one of the VM's recovery points, before the start of the incident: the isolation, or the first detection of its pending card when earlier (`restore_plan.incident_start`, the rule of `--before`).
  - The VM running.
  - **The neutralization, by the activity log**: the last succeeded Run Command on the VM before the job started was made by a principal that restores (`GLORFINDEL_RESTORE_PRINCIPALS`, in H0's `host.env`), and none by anyone else since. Then, one more condition and never enough alone: the guest's word after the restore is the harmless output. The guest writes it; an attacker's script can print the public marker, and root can rewrite it.
  - Then the watch builds its own `recovery_complete`, with the same fields as `restore --wait` and vouched for in its process, and hands it to the VM's queue. The release follows as on a workstation: autonomous where the mode allows, a `mode_hold` card in `human_only`, the release precondition unchanged. Without the activity log's fact, the neutralization is unknown and the release is held.
  - A failed job, a restore not in place, no container nor named point, a point not before the incident or unreadable: one `release_hold` card (action `review_recovery`, nothing to approve in one click), no release. A VM not running 10 min after its job ended: one card, and the release follows once it runs.
  - Once per job (`~/.glorfindel/recoveries.json`, written before the signal leaves; the records of a VM no longer isolated dropped). A release running, a restore claimed on this host, or a job ended less than `GLORFINDEL_RECOVERY_GRACE_S` (300 s) ago: left alone, or waits.
  - Reads only, under the Reader role H0 already holds on the bench's group, where the vault is. Unmeasured on the bench: the job's « Job Type » and how it names its point, the caller id the activity log gives the console's principal, and the guest's word after a restored disk boots. Any of them missing gives a card, never a release.
  - Tests: `tests/unit/test_recovery_watch.py`, on the fake Azure. `tests/unit/fake_azure.py` now carries a vault's restore jobs and their type, recovery points, power state, OS disks and their creation, the activity log and its callers, and the guest's word. Its state machine has a hostile guest and an attacker printing the marker. R1: never a `recovery_complete` without a recovery proven on Azure. R2: never two for one job, nor two cards for one job and one reason. Also `tests/unit/test_glorfindel_host.py` (the principals reach the watch alone). Mutants M4615–M4619, M4628–M4632 (`scripts/mutants/REC1.py`).
