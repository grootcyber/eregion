### Changed
- **The invariant machine reads what holds an isolation or a block in its own journal, never in the code's marks** (RSM2b, #1034; `docs/design/reaffirmation.md`, §5, E3). No change to the product. The reassertion leaves alone an isolation or a block an operation holds: a release or an unblock under way, one cut off, one partial. Its oracles (L1, L3, the bypass) read that hold in the code's own marks (`partial`, `release_failed`, `releasing_at`, `unblock_failed`, `unblocking_at`), so a defect that set one wrongly would have silenced them for that VM, unseen. As the maintainer decided on 21/11 (Q3), the machine now notes each operation it sees, wherever it comes from (a step, the reassertion, a race, the CLI): its answer, its error, and its writes of the VM's local state that landed.
  - A hold begins with an operation that raised an error or gave an answer that is not whole (`release_partial`, `unblock_partial`, `swept_partial`, an isolation or a block raised partial), once a write of its VM's state landed. A fault alone begins nothing: one the code swallows, followed by a whole answer, ends the hold, and none is needed for one to begin. A hold ends when the test sees it written whole or lifted (`isolated`, `released`, `swept`, `blocked`, `unblocked`, `not_found`), or when the test destroys the state. A perimeter block's `blocked_partial` names NICs off its subnet, not a write cut short: it ends the hold, as the code writes it whole, whether its scope comes by name or third, as the queue and the console's gestures pass it.
  - Two leniencies, each making the oracle demand less, never more. An isolation or a block cut off right after it wrote its state or its entry whole holds too, with no mark in the code, until its next whole write or lift (in `human_only`, often the rest of the sequence): the test can't tell a whole state from a partial one without the mark. And in a race, what ends a hold is applied before what begins one.
  - That first leniency is a limit, not a gap: such a cut-off is always a crash, every state it leaves a sequence without an error also reaches, which L1 judges, and the review found no mutant of the product that passes through that window alone. What is lost is sampling. A way to narrow it for an isolation is written in the note, not taken: as it stands, it is not safe.
  - The journal of the unblocks the test saw superseded by a whole block (`reblocked`, L1B) goes: a whole block now ends the hold itself.
  - Measured both ways: a mark a defect sets on a whole isolation or block exempts nothing; a hold the product rightly keeps (an isolation partial on its own, a release cut off right after its intent) is exempt by the journal; a hold written whole since is judged again. M13 and M5321 stay caught.
  - Two points of RSM2a's review: the dating of what a lift can no longer reach (`_loose`) is now kept by a frozen case (the state lost, then a whole block, its entry taken off by hand, the NSG taken off its subnet: still in reach); the other one, a card recognised by a substring, was closed by RSM3, which removed that excuse.
  - Tests: `tests/unit/test_invariants.py`, `tests/unit/test_invariant_journal.py`, `tests/unit/test_invariant_hold.py`. Mutants M7050–M7053, M8126 and M8127 (`scripts/mutants/RSM2b.py`).
