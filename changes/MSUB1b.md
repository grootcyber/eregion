### Changed
- **The design of a watch over several subscriptions is settled: an added subscription is treated like the watch's own** (MSUB1b, #1206; `docs/design/watch-multi-abonnement.md`). The maintainer answered the twelve questions on 25/11 and set a principle for the whole note: an added subscription (a Dev, a Preprod, a Staging) gets no regime of its own, while inheriting nothing (its VMs stay `human_only` until its entry says otherwise) and leaving its reach only once nothing is held there. Four answers depart from the note's recommendation, and the note says so. Once a subscription is in reach, the console's and the bot's buttons write there as in the watch's own, and its VMs act alone by their mode after the readiness check; on the hosted watch, where the console and the bot share the VM's identity, their blast radius therefore extends to every subscription in reach until each container has its own identity, which the threat model now says. The subscription guard compares a target with the declared list for the watch, the console and the bot alike. No write is ever allowed outside a subscription in reach: withdrawing it is refused while an isolation or a block is held there, and said; a right removed in Azure while a state is held is said on the card, and the release goes through the CLI. Every role moves to Terraform everywhere, the bench included, the console's and the bot's too, after a one-time import of each assignment made by hand: a plan always shows them, where an apply used to leave the response role alone. The session drain stays VM by VM in Terraform, as in the watch's own subscription, and the restore follows the same rule: the console and the bot restore with their roles, also declared in Terraform. The vault is the user's, declared in the configuration and never deployed by Eregion; for each VM the watch finds, read-only, the vault that protects it, and without one the snapshot is refused and said while the isolation still happens. The bench measure uses a new dedicated subscription of the same tenant, not the one kept for the attack library. The note adds the review's precisions (the trigger of the private-address fix, kept active once another subscription is seen; the watch's own subscription refused in the list; the session drain's missing write guard; the six places the autonomy is resolved by name), drops the ones the answers made moot (undoing outside the reach, a console held back while the action queue is off), and revises the lots and their sizes (about 25 days). No code changes.
