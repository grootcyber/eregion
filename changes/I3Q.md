When the verification that a perimeter block makes right after its write concludes `False` (a NIC proven open, or a block of ours bypassed), `_block_ip_subnet` took off its list the NICs proven held (`blocked_nics`, `held_by_perimeter`) and kept every other NIC off the perimeter « not behind it, {ip} still reaches them » (`uncovered_nics`). Among them were the NICs the verification couldn't read: their rules, their subnet or their perimeter answered 429 (`nics_unknown`, `perimeter_unread`), or the VM's NICs couldn't be listed. The claim was positive and made without a read. The answer also held one key only, `uncovered_nics` or `nics_unread`, never both. Jonathan decided it on 12/11: say them unread. The block now sorts the NICs off its perimeter into three kinds. The NICs proven held stay off the lists, as before. The NICs proven open (`open_nics`: nothing of ours blocks them) go in `uncovered_nics`, with « still reaches them ». The others go in `nics_unread`, each with its name and why (« db-nic-0 (périmètre illisible) »), with « unread … whether {ip} reaches them is unverified ». Both keys can be said together, the note says both, and the status stays `blocked_partial` whenever a NIC is open or unread, never `blocked`. `nics_unread` stays a string, as in the branch `None`, so the console and the drift card read it unchanged. The console's promotion window already says both keys, each on its own line; a test now holds it. The machine's oracle doesn't change: it already reads the two keys together (`said`), a NIC said unread makes no claim, and a NIC said reached is judged against the world. No excuse was added to it (A0). I3P holds: an unread NIC is never said covered, and its test now expects `nics_unread`, no longer `uncovered_nics`.
