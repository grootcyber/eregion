### Fixed
- **A perimeter block whose verification a 429 cut short no longer says « the IP still reaches » a NIC that another perimeter of ours cuts off** (I3N, #746; `glorfindel/providers/azure/blocks.py`, `docs/azure.md`, `docs/design/preuves.md`). The night « Invariants (deep) » of 12/11 (run 43207966449, on 90f00faf, 10 000 examples) went red on macOS alone: « I3: perimeter block named ['db-nic-0'] of …/Db--01 uncovered; our perimeter on their subnet cuts them off ». The setup: Db--01's primary NIC on the data subnet, web blocked on the app subnet, then Db--01 blocked on its own subnet, its 13th call answered 429. That call is the read of nsg-data that the block's verification makes to check the pair it has just written. **The defect was the product's, not the oracle's.** When the verification can't conclude (`verified: None`: any of its eight reads in 429, or the verification raising), `_block_ip_subnet` kept every NIC off the perimeter in `uncovered_nics`, « not behind it, the IP still reaches them », `blocked_partial`, and dropped the error. db-nic-0 sits on the app subnet, behind web's perimeter, which cuts the attacker off, and nothing about it had been read. The claim was positive and made without a read, so the oracle (`_perimeter_cuts`) was right to judge it against the world: no excuse was added to it, and I3 is unchanged. Such a block now says its NICs unread (`nics_unread`, with the verification's error), `blocked_partial`: it never says them uncovered, and it never says `blocked` on an unknown (I3P). The next verification, once it can read, tells which: held by the other perimeter (`held_by_perimeter`) or uncovered. **Neither macOS nor case was the cause.** The same draws fail on Linux, and so does the id in lowercase. Db--01 is the machine's only VM whose second NIC can sit off its perimeter. The state files are already named by the lowercased name plus a hash of the id. In CI the machine is derandomized and the set it draws depends on the system (MUT4): the night drew the sequence on macOS.
  - **Two neighbors, found while looking for them, and red on the same oracle.** Both concern a NIC whose subnet couldn't be read (a 429): its views then hold no NSG of its subnet. First, `verify_block_ip`. Db--01 blocked for itself, then on its subnet, gives one merged entry. A 429 on the read of db-nic-0's subnet then made its own block's placement, which lies on that subnet's NSG, read as « nsg-subnet ne gouverne plus db-nic-0 ». The rule was counted missing, the NIC open and uncovered, behind two blocks of ours, and the reassertion would have put the rules back on a guess. That NIC is now unknown (« NSG du subnet illisible »), unless another placement proves it blocked. Second, `_perimeter_holds` read a perimeter on such a NIC as « no », and the NIC was said open. It now answers « unknown ». `verify_isolation`, the release, the unblock and the reset already read this flag (`subnet_unreadable`); the block's verification did not.
