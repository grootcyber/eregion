### Fixed
- **`glorfindel reset --from-azure` also takes off a block on the NSG its entry names, once the customer took that NSG off the subnet** (REL068, the 0.6.8 release gate; `docs/azure.md`). A VM was blocked on its subnet's NSG, then the customer took that NSG off the subnet or put another in its place. The reset read only the NSGs the NICs carry and the isolation state's placements. It said `swept`, took nothing off, and cleared the block entry that named the NSG, so the block's two rules stayed there with nothing left to trace them; they come back into force if the customer puts the NSG back. The unblock was right already, since it goes where the entry's placements put the rules. The reset now also reads the NSG each placement of a block entry names, when the NIC no longer carries it. The same guards hold:
  - only our rules are taken off, by their names and the NIC's hash, never a customer's rule;
  - an NSG that cannot be read is a failure (`swept_partial`, the entry kept and marked);
  - an NSG deleted since has no rules left, so the entry is cleared;
  - `--dry-run` names the rules where they lie and writes nothing.

  The defect predates the invariant machine's ability to take a subnet's NSG off, which is how it was reached.
  - Tests: `tests/unit/test_release_away.py` (five cases, three red before the fix); `tests/unit/test_invariant_regressions.py` freezes the gate's sequence (23 steps reduced to 3), red before the fix with the gate's message. Mutant M7714 (`scripts/mutants/REL068.py`).
- **Our rules on an NSG taken off a subnet and given to another VM are out of the unblock's reach once nothing traces them, as the invariant machine now judges** (REL068, the 0.6.8 release gate; `docs/design/reaffirmation.md`, E7). The gate's other I7 was the oracle's. A VM was blocked on its subnet's replacement NSG and the block was cut by a fault before its entry. A new VM was then given that NSG on its own NIC, and the NSG was taken off the first VM's subnet. Nothing of the first VM names that NSG and none of its NICs carries it, so the unblock never goes there. The oracle exempted such rules only on an NSG that governs nothing at all. An NSG taken off a subnet is now out of reach for a VM when it governs none of that VM's NICs. A NIC of the VM with no NSG at all keeps the stricter judgement, since the unblock may look for its rules on every NSG.
  - Tests: `tests/unit/test_invariant_regressions.py` freezes the sequence (29 steps reduced to 4), red on `main` with the gate's message and green with the fix. It also freezes its other side: a bare NIC's rules stay in reach, and an unblock made to forget its sweep fails I7. Mutants M7712–M7713 (`scripts/mutants/REL068.py`); RSM2a's M7463 re-anchored.
