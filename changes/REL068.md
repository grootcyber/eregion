### Fixed
- **Our rules on an NSG taken off a subnet and given to another VM are out of the unblock's reach once nothing traces them, as the invariant machine now judges** (REL068, the 0.6.8 release gate; `docs/design/reaffirmation.md`, E7). The gate (2 000 sequences) was red on two I7 counterexamples, replayed on Linux and reduced step by step. The first is the oracle's: a VM blocked on its subnet's replacement NSG, the block cut by a fault before its entry, a new VM given that NSG on its own NIC, then the NSG taken off the first VM's subnet. Nothing of the first VM names that NSG and none of its NICs carries it, so the unblock never goes there; the oracle exempted such rules only on an NSG that governs nothing at all. An NSG taken off a subnet is now out of reach for a VM when it governs none of that VM's NICs; a NIC of the VM with no NSG at all keeps the stricter judgement, since the unblock may look for its rules on every NSG. The product is unchanged. The second counterexample is a defect of the product, not fixed here: `glorfindel reset --from-azure` says `swept`, removes nothing and clears a block entry whose NSG, taken off the subnet, it never read, leaving the two rules with no trace (three steps; the same on the code before the machine could take a subnet's NSG off).
  - Tests: `tests/unit/test_invariant_regressions.py` freezes the first sequence (29 steps reduced to 4), red on `main` with the gate's message and green with the fix, and its other side, a bare NIC's rules kept in reach (the unblock made to forget its sweep fails I7). Mutants M7712–M7713 (`scripts/mutants/REL068.py`); RSM2a's M7463 re-anchored.
