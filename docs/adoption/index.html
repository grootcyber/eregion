Eregion, for those who judge it

Cloud detection and response for teams without a SOC, and the proof that it works. One sheet per reader: what they fear, what changes for them, three proofs, what to show them in five minutes, their objection and the next step.

How to read the figures. Every figure on this page comes from one file of the repository, docs/proofs.json, with its date and its source: a page of the repository, the sentence of it that gives the figure, and the heading it sits under or the run it comes from. The date is the day or the month of the measure or, when the source dates no measure, the day the source states it. A test refuses a figure without its date or its source.

Most are measured on the project's own bench, on small samples. An estimate, a setting of the code, or a measure to take again says so beside its date. The documents the sources point to are mostly in French.

What we do not promise

  • Azure virtual machines, today. AWS and GCP: a provider interface is planned, nothing more.
  • Identities and the control plane: detection only. Azure's Activity Log and Entra ID's logs are watched, and every detection goes to a human. On an identity, the card carries a plan and a check read again on Microsoft Graph; a human applies it. No action on an identity is written yet.
  • Not a SOC. On-call humans and workstations stay with a team or a managed SOC.
  • full_auto is planned, not available: the configuration refuses it. Autonomy today is non_disruptive, granted VM by VM once its readiness is checked: reversible actions alone, a destructive one always waiting for a human. The default is human_only: nothing runs without a human.
  • Autonomy on a real fleet is not recommended yet. The criteria the project set before it are not all met — among them, four weeks of observation on a real fleet. Autonomy runs on the bench.
  • The ransomware times come from a June run, before the hosted watch, Orodruin and Dagorlad; they will be replayed. With the hosted watch, the restore is started from the operator's machine, and lifting the isolation after it is a second human step today. Making the watch see the restore itself is planned (#668).
  • A log line the monitored VM can write itself is not yet corroborated before an autonomous action on it; that corroboration is planned (threat model, the model provider).
  • Bench measures, on small samples — a single measure of the emergency stop, a single fault so far. They show that the mechanism works; they are not statistics.
  • No hosted offer, no multi-tenant console, no price. Eregion runs on your machine or in your VM.

The objection

What if the AI gets it wrong?

It chooses among planned actions, and a series of guards in code follows: under its confidence threshold it escalates; a disruptive action taken alone needs several draws of the model to agree; a model, a cloud API or a telemetry that fails gives the hand back to a human, never to the action.

The model decides. The code checks.

The fear: a black box that acts on production.

What changes

  • To respond, the model is asked one thing: which planned action, with what confidence. Detecting, investigating, guarding, acting and verifying are code, tested without a cloud and without a model.
  • The model is chosen by measure, in a harness of decision cases, and changed by one setting; the provider is yours to pick, a local model included.
  • It fails closed: when the model, the cloud API, the telemetry or its own configuration fails, nothing disruptive runs alone, and a card says what was missing.

Show, in five minutes

Glorfindel from the inside: one cycle, from the log line to the action verified against Azure's real state, in its decision file.

The objection

And the day the model changes?

The harness is replayed before the change, overconfidence included; on the reference host, a new lock of dependencies deploys only with the harness's signed attestation. Eregion trains no model on your data.

What changes

  • When an attack slips through, Glorfindel proposes a rule grounded in the real schema of your log tables; it runs only once you approve it, and then deterministically.
  • Targeted queries investigate before each decision; the card says what was asked and what came back.
  • Public ATT&CK techniques (Atomic Red Team, pinned), replayed from a dedicated host on enrolled targets only; injection through the logs and through the memory is part of the harness.

Show, in five minutes

A full cycle in the console: the detection, the investigation, the card to judge.

The objection

I want my own rules.

They live in your own layer, which an update never overwrites; the shipped base stays intact, and a rule of yours can override or turn off one of its rules. Sharing a rule back to the base — with your consent, generalised, stripped of identifiers — is planned, not built.

SRE · platform

It does not cut what must keep running.

The fear: a tool that isolates a healthy VM in the middle of the night.

0 / 1declared fault taken for an attack, on the bench (a single fault so far)31 Oct 2026 · ROADMAP.md
< 3 sfrom revoking the injectors' host identity to its first refused call, the attack side's emergency stop (one measure)19 Oct 2026 · docs/design/orodruin-contract.md

What changes

  • Restraint is measured: declared faults (a VM stopped, disk writes, CPU load) are played to check that Glorfindel does not take them for attacks, and a bench VM with real crons, updates and backups runs under its eye.
  • Every autonomous action is reversible and verified on the cloud API; a release brings each VM back to its design, which property tests check against random customer networks.
  • Isolation coexists with your infrastructure code: Glorfindel writes only what is its own, and alerts when something changes outside it.

Show, in five minutes

A declared fault: a VM stopped, no isolation, and the return to normal checked.

The objection

What if it breaks anyway?

Read-only by default; autonomy is granted VM by VM, and taken back in one click. Every isolation and every block can be released. On the attack side, one command stops every injector: it revokes their identity and stops their host.

What leaves is said in full; the rest stays with you.

The fear: production data sent to a model, subprocessors, a retention nobody can state.

What changes

  • What goes to the model, at each decision: the detection's log lines (VM names, addresses, user and process names, as the logs carry them), the investigation's results, the incident's context, a few similar past decisions and the VM's isolation state. Identity and control-plane detections are decided by the code: nothing of them goes to the model.
  • The memory and its embeddings run on your host. Eregion sends no telemetry to its authors, and trains no model on your data.
  • The provider is yours to choose, under your own contract; or a local model, so that the logs never leave — measured less reliable in the harness: keep it read-only.

Show, in five minutes

The threat model's page on the model provider, and the message the model is actually sent, as the code builds it.

The objection

A model outside our jurisdiction?

It is replaced by one setting — a provider under your contract, in your region, or a local model — and every model goes through the same harness before it decides.

What changes

  • Each campaign leaves dated evidence: what ran, what was detected, what was decided, and how long it took.
  • The console says the age of the last proof of each technique, and turns it red when it is too old.
  • The report's figures come from files, never from a model, which only explains them. Eregion is not a compliance product: its evidence is there to be read.

Show, in five minutes

The objection

Who guarantees the report tells the truth?

Its figures are computed from the runs' files; the watch's heartbeat and its detections are signed; the code and the threat model are public, and a vulnerability is reported as SECURITY.md says.
